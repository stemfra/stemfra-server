# Stemfra SWOT (2026-09-17)

_Written from evidence we hold, not from hope: the market research on about 40 booking providers
(`MARKET_RESEARCH_PROVIDERS_2026-09.md`), our own lead-gen data (Places), Neil's Barbershop call,
the failed cold cohort, and what is built and live today. Where a point is a judgment rather than a
fact it says so. Status of the company: pre-revenue, no paying tenant yet, a complete product, a
working lead engine, a small remote team._

## 1. Strengths (internal, true today)

1. **The only offer in the field that is a real branded website, built and run for the owner.**
   Across about 40 tools, the premium platforms (Boulevard, Mangomint, Phorest, Meevo, Zenoti) sell
   a widget, page or app; marketplaces give a profile; websites are a paid add-on at Vagaro,
   StyleSeat, Jane, Zen Planner, PushPress, Walla; only Wix gives a full site and the owner builds
   it. Ours comes with the domain, the design for the vertical, the copy and the upkeep.
2. **No marketplace.** The owner's clients see only the owner. On Booksy, Fresha, Vagaro, StyleSeat,
   Treatwell, Mindbody and MassageBook the shop sits beside competitors and pays 15% to 35% on a
   new client's first visit. This is the argument owners raise themselves in reviews.
3. **Nothing to pay to start and nothing in a quiet month.** No setup, no subscription, no per-seat
   fee, no add-on price list, no contract, data leaves with them. Every competitor with a contract
   (Mindbody, Booker, Phorest, Meevo, Zenoti, Boulevard, Glofox, WellnessLiving) collects complaints
   about exactly that.
4. **The product is finished and broad for its price point:** six vertical designs with themes,
   booking for teams, classes and memberships, reminders, client import, win-back and review
   requests, AI front desk (chat), Stacy the setup copilot, own-domain purchase and email
   forwarding, custom hostnames at scale, CMS with tutorials and a Help Center.
5. **A low-cost operating base.** Remote team, mostly fixed costs near zero per tenant
   (Cloudflare, Supabase, one VPS); AI-assisted build speed (today alone: a native lead-gen engine,
   a Places dataset, provider scripts).
6. **Our own demand engine and data.** Native lead-gen keeps every Google Maps place with its
   rating, review count, booking platform and readiness (about $0.02 a place); provider-tagged
   leads open the right pitch; the CRM carries calling, texting, scripts, compliance gates (PECR,
   CASL, do-not-call) and work-time tracking. Few seed-stage companies have this.
7. **Pay at the venue.** Nothing to connect, no payout holds (a live Fresha, GlossGenius and Square
   complaint), no processor markup from us, live the same day.
8. **A price with a ceiling (decided 2026-09-17): 5% of bookings, never more than $400 a month.**
   Below the cap the owner pays in proportion to what the site actually books, and nothing in a
   month with no bookings; above $8,000 of monthly bookings the bill stops growing. That puts our
   worst case ($400) inside what owners already pay for premium tools (Boulevard $143 to $328,
   Mangomint $160 to $190, Phorest about $150 to $300, Mindbody about $279 to $500+, Zenoti $440
   to $540, WellnessLiving $199 to $500, PushPress full stack $488 to $559, Zen Planner $616), with
   the website, the domain, the whole team, win-back, reviews and the AI front desk included, no
   contract and no add-on list. Neil's call (2026-09-16) confirmed owners think in the $200 to
   $400 a month range. No competitor we researched offers "free until you are booked, capped when
   you are busy".
9. **Founder selling.** Peter makes the calls himself and turns each one into product and script
   changes the same day (Neil's call → old-school exclusion, win-back-first story).

## 2. Weaknesses (internal, honest)

1. **No paying customer and no proof.** No case study, no review of Stemfra, no number for bookings
   brought in, win-back recovered or reviews gained. Every claim in the pitch is a promise. This
   is the weakness that outweighs the others.
2. **Price: the cap fixed the worst of it; three things remain.** (Was: "price compares badly
   exactly where our target is": uncapped, 5% of $30,000 is $1,500 against $40 to $230 on
   mainstream tools. The $400 cap, decided 2026-09-17, removes that.) What is left: (a) a mid-size
   shop booking $8,000+ a month pays $400 where GlossGenius is $56, Square $49 to $64, Booksy about
   $110 and Vagaro $64 to $120, so against the cheap tools the pitch must still rest on the site,
   the growth tools and the service, never on price; (b) the cap's terms are not yet written down
   everywhere: per location, and whether at-visit sales marked collected count toward it (assumed
   yes to both, to confirm); (c) the cap is not yet in the code or the public pages: the commission
   meter, the Fees page, Pricing, the FAQ, the claim email line and the call scripts still say a
   flat 5%. Membership-heavy gyms and studios reach the cap almost at once (150 members at $150 =
   $22,500), so for them we are in effect a $400 a month product: competitive with their $180 to
   $650 stacks, but it should be pitched that way.
3. **Commission collection rests on trust.** Pay-at-venue means we invoice 5% of sales the owner
   marks collected (auto-collected after 24 hours). There is no payment rail enforcing it, no
   deposits against no-shows, and under-reporting or disputes are untested. The Airwallex
   auto-split path was ruled out on cost.
4. **The switch is a bigger ask than we make it sound.** Two steps for the owner, but in practice:
   staff retraining, client habits, upcoming appointments, packages, gift cards and memberships in
   the old tool, and the loss of marketplace-sourced new clients for shops that do get them. We
   have a CSV client import; we have no appointment, package or membership migration, and the
   import presets were guesses until today's hardening.
5. **Feature gaps against mature tools:** no native mobile app for owners or clients, no POS or
   retail inventory, no payroll or commission splits, no deposits or card on file (pay-at-venue),
   no two-way calendar sync, voice agent inbound only. Against Mangomint, Boulevard or PushPress an
   owner gives up things they use daily.
6. **Tiny team, founder dependency, thin sales capacity.** Sales managers are not yet hired; the
   reps will call the US, Canada and the UK from Nigeria on evening shifts; a caller ID that reads
   "Felton, DE"; no in-person visits by design.
7. **Outbound has not worked yet.** The first cold cohort reached 3% and the sequencer is off;
   Twilio restricted the account once over our own test traffic; cold texts are illegal in all
   three markets, cold email to UK sole traders and most Canadian contacts needs consent.
8. **The ideal lead is scarce, and mostly not on a marketplace.** Our own data (671 places, five
   cities, 2026-09-17): 29% already own a website and are out by decision; 119 places are busy
   (200+ reviews at 4.5+) and 76 of those own a site, leaving 42 busy shops with no storefront
   (6% of what we scrape). Of the 333 places that became leads only 26 (8%) show a booking tool;
   the typical lead is an established shop that takes bookings by phone, walk-in or Instagram.
   So the "no storefront" pitch (A1b) is our main pitch and the marketplace pitch (A1) the
   minority one. And 247 of 248 New Leads have a phone but only 13 have an email: this is a
   calling business, not an email one.
9. **Brand and trust are at zero** in markets where owners have been burned by software vendors,
   and where a foreign, unknown company asks to become their storefront.
10. **Process debt we found in ourselves this week:** a subscription-era prompt lived in production
    for weeks; the site promised "runs alongside your current system" in 8 places and card
    payments in 12 while the strategy said otherwise. Fixed, but it shows how fast copy and
    strategy drift apart without a single source of truth.

## 3. Opportunities (external)

1. **Owners are angry at marketplaces right now.** Fresha ended "free forever" and charges 20%;
   Booksy Boost 30% and StyleSeat 30% are charged on clients owners say were already theirs;
   StyleSeat's $2.35 client fee cuts tips; Treatwell takes 35% in the UK; Vagaro added a 20% fee.
   Each has a named, documented complaint we can ask about.
2. **Forced migrations create ready switchers:** Schedulicity was folded into Vagaro in January
   2025; Square raised $29 plans to $49 in October 2025; Mindbody / Booker renewals and price
   jumps ($85 to $599 in one report) recur every year.
3. **Contract calendars.** Phorest, Zenoti, Meevo, Booker, Mindbody and WellnessLiving owners can
   only leave in a window. A CRM field for the renewal month turns that into a dated pipeline.
4. ~~The "busy shop with a weak site on a marketplace" segment~~ Closed 2026-09-17 (Peter): shops that
   already own and pay for a website are not our target. The data stays in Places.
5. **Public data nobody in this niche uses:** Texas, New York and Florida license files give owner
   names; Companies House gives UK directors and tells us who may legally be emailed; the CrossFit
   affiliate map lists about 9,900 owner-operated gyms.
6. **Partners with concentrated owners:** salon-suite operators (Sola 750+ locations, Phenix 400+,
   independents), distributor field reps, barber educators, salon accountants. One relationship
   can open dozens of owner-operators.
7. **Paid social that lands on a claim page.** Peter's own experience ($0.35 clicks, high sign-up
   when there is no friction) against a product that is free to claim. Prerequisites known:
   funnel tracking and a frictionless "See my site" flow.
8. **Google's own shift.** Business Profile chat is gone, reviews and the booking link on the
   profile matter more; our win-back → reviews → Maps visibility loop sits exactly there, and a
   public review count is proof an owner can see without trusting our numbers.
9. **AI as a wedge owners can feel:** an AI front desk and voice agent included, while Fresha
   sells its AI concierge at $99.95 a location and Zenoti gates AI behind a higher tier.
10. **UK and Canada are less crowded for an own-site offer;** Treatwell's 35% and Timely's per-staff
    pricing leave room, and Companies House makes UK targeting precise.

## 4. Threats (external)

1. **Incumbents can copy the visible part fast.** Vagaro, GlossGenius, DaySmart, Rosy and Square
   already sell or bundle website builders; a marketplace can let owners hide competitors or drop
   the new-client fee the day churn hurts. Our edge must be the done-for-you service and the growth
   results, not the feature list.
2. **Well-funded, well-liked competitors:** GlossGenius (about $100M revenue, 125,000 businesses),
   Boulevard ($80M Series D), Mangomint (4.9 on Capterra, month to month, free migration), Square
   (free plan, hardware lock-in), PushPress (free tier for gyms). A content owner on these has
   little reason to move.
3. **The 5% becomes the story.** One owner doing the math in a Facebook group can define us before we
   have defenders. The cap is the answer ("never more than $400"), which is why it must be on every
   public surface before outreach scales. Competitors' blogs already attack each other
   this way.
4. **Platform and channel risk:** Twilio (already restricted once), Google Maps data via Apify,
   Meta ad accounts, Cloudflare, Supabase; Booksy and Fresha ban scraping and vendor solicitation;
   Facebook groups and SalonGeek ban pitches; Instagram throttles cold DMs.
5. **Regulation:** TCPA (no cold texts, mobile "shop numbers"), CASL (opt-in, DMs included), PECR
   (sole traders), GDPR on director names, sales-tax nexus as revenue appears, and the cross-border
   structure (Delaware LLC, founder abroad) under tax review.
6. **Marketplace dependence of the prospect.** A shop that really gets new clients from Booksy or
   ClassPass loses that flow when it switches; if our win-back and reviews do not replace it within
   a couple of months, it churns and says so publicly.
7. **Revenue leakage and disputes** once real money flows on an honour-based basis; a few early
   non-payers would cost more in founder time than in dollars.
8. **Macro:** discretionary services fall first in a downturn, and a commission model falls with
   them, while subscription competitors keep billing.
9. **Execution risk of a very small team** across four products (platform, CRM, marketing site, AI
   agents): an outage, a bad migration or a slow support reply in the first ten clients would be
   remembered.

## 5. What the SWOT says to do (in order)

1. **Close the proof gap before anything else (W1).** Get five to ten founding clients on
   generous terms (for example the first 90 days free of commission, or a low fixed cap, in
   exchange for a case study, a review and their numbers). Pick them from the strongest segment:
   busy barbers and salons on Booksy, Fresha, StyleSeat, Vagaro; ex-Schedulicity owners; UK shops
   on Treatwell. Measure three numbers per client: bookings through the site, clients won back,
   Google reviews gained.
2. ~~Decide the price shape~~ **DECIDED 2026-09-17: 5% of bookings, never more than $400 a month.**
   Now carry it through: the commission meter (cap per site per month), the Fees page, Pricing,
   the FAQ ("Is 5% more than I pay now?" gets a far better answer), the claim email line, A1 / A1b,
   the call scripts ("do not promise a cap" comes out), the decks and Master Plan v3. Confirm the
   two terms: per location, and at-visit sales count toward the cap.
3. **Make the switch real, not just short (W4).** Test the import on genuine Booksy, Fresha,
   Vagaro, Square, GlossGenius and Mindbody exports (research in progress); add upcoming
   appointments to the migration; publish "How to export your data from …" in the Help Center;
   offer to do the Google profile link swap with the owner on a call.
4. ~~Open the larger segment (O4, W8)~~ **Decided against, 2026-09-17 (Peter):** shops that already own
   and pay for a website are not our target, even when they book through a marketplace. They stay
   in Places as market data.
5. **Work the calendar, not just the list (O3):** add the renewal month to the lead, ask it on
   every call with a contract vendor, and follow up 60 to 90 days before.
6. **Protect collection early (W3, T7):** clear monthly statements, the owner confirms the total in
   the CMS, a simple rule for disputes, and optional deposits later.
7. **One source of truth for the offer (W10):** the positioning in `GTM_PLAN` section 10 drives the
   site, the templates, the scripts, the prompts and the decks; any change starts there and is
   then searched for across all surfaces (the way the coexist lines were found today).
8. **Start two low-cost channels while calls build proof (O5, O6, O7):** the Texas / New York owner
   files joined to Places for first-name calls, and two or three suite operators or educators for
   referrals. Hold the $300 ad pilot until the claim flow is frictionless and tracked.
