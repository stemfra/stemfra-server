# Pricing comparison (updated 2026-09-17)

_First read 2026-09-15 (vendor pages), rebuilt 2026-09-17 with the wider provider research
(`MARKET_RESEARCH_PROVIDERS_2026-09.md`, every figure sourced there) and **the decided Stemfra
price: a flat 5% of bookings, never more than $400 a month**. Worked example throughout: one shop,
3 staff, 80 card visits a month averaging $100 = $8,000 a month. "All-in" = software + card
processing, because that is the bill the owner feels. Figures marked 3P come from third parties;
re-check on the vendor's page before quoting._

## 2. What the table says

- **Below the cap we track the mainstream; at the cap we sit with the premium tools; above it our
  rate falls while theirs does not rise either.** At $8,000 a month the mainstream tools cost an
  owner about $250 all-in and we cost $400 plus their terminal. That gap is the price of what
  only we include: a real website on their own domain, built and run for them, the whole team,
  win-back, review requests, the AI front desk, no add-on list, no contract.
- **"You get a website" is no longer unique; "we build and run it, and it is only yours" is.**
  Six tools now include or sell a site: GlossGenius and Square include a DIY booking website,
  MassageBook a simple one, Wix is a DIY builder, Fresha (Smart Website) and Vagaro (MySite, $20 a
  month) sell one as an extra. All of them are build-it-yourself, and the marketplace ones still
  keep the shop's marketplace page live beside it. Booksy, StyleSeat, Treatwell, Mindbody,
  Boulevard, Mangomint, Phorest, Meevo, Zenoti, Setmore and Acuity give a profile page or a widget
  and no website. The fitness tools charge $99 to $329 a month extra for one. Pitch the done-for-you
  build, their own domain and "nobody else on the page", never just "a free website".
- **Who each platform is built for decides who we meet on calls.** Barbers: Booksy, theCut, Squire,
  Setmore, Square. Salons: Fresha, Vagaro, GlossGenius, StyleSeat, Square; premium: Boulevard,
  Mangomint, Phorest, Meevo. UK: Fresha, Treatwell, Phorest, Timely. Yoga / Pilates / fitness:
  Mindbody, WellnessLiving, Momence, Wodify, PushPress, Zen Planner. Massage: MassageBook, Jane,
  ClinicSense, Noterro, Vagaro.
- **A dead month costs nothing with us and the full fee with all of them.** Nobody else in this
  table can say that except the free solo tiers (Square, Setmore), which carry no website and no
  growth tools.
- **Marketplace owners pay twice:** a subscription AND 15% to 35% of each new client's first visit
  (Booksy, Fresha, Vagaro, StyleSeat, Treatwell, Mindbody, MassageBook), while their page sits next
  to competitors. That is where "your clients should only see you" lands hardest.
- **Be straight about card processing.** Clients pay at the venue, so we add no processing fee, but
  the owner still pays their own terminal (about 2.6%). Say "we add no payment fees on top of
  yours", never "no payment fees".
- **Fitness and membership businesses hit the cap at once** (150 members at $150 = $22,500), so for
  them Stemfra is in effect a $400 a month product: against stacks of $390 to $860 that is
  competitive, and it should be pitched as a flat, all-included price.

## 3. History: the read on 10% (rejected 2026-09-15)

A flat 10% was considered and rejected: the mainstream tools land at 2.7% to 3.5% all-in, the high
marketplace rates (20% to 35%) are one-time fees on clients the platform brought in, and nobody
charges 10% on a shop's own regulars. At $8,000 a month 10% would have been $800. The idea that
survives from that discussion is a PERFORMANCE fee (a higher rate only on bookings Stemfra itself
generates: win-back, reviews, AI front desk), parked as a possible growth lever, not a plan.

## 4. Decisions

- 2026-09-15: 5% stays, 10% rejected.
- 2026-09-16: no cap for now (superseded).
- **2026-09-17: 5% of bookings, capped at $400 a month.** Reasons: the target is busy shops, where
  an uncapped 5% was the one number an owner could not accept ($1,500 on $30,000); owners already
  pay $200 to $400 a month (Neil's call); $200 was judged too low for us. Terms confirmed by Peter
  the same day: the cap is per location; at-visit sales count toward it, and only sales from
  bookings made through our website count at all (walk-ins carry no commission). On hold by
  Peter's word: not in the billing code or the public pages yet (ROADMAP "PRICING DECIDED
  2026-09-17").
